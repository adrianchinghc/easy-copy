# Page types

Templates for the pages beyond the home page, taken from outrank.so and squad.so. Use their shape, not their sentence length. Every line still has to pass the third-grade rules and the Step 4b check in `SKILL.md`. Never fill a slot with invented proof. If a slot needs proof you don't have, leave it out.

## Use-case or feature page (one job per page)

This is the strongest page type for a product that does several jobs. Squad has one page per job ("Invoice chaser", "Inbox triage").

1. **Eyebrow:** category · when it runs. → "Use cases · Money · Weekdays at 9:00 AM"
2. **H1, outcome plus "without you":** `[Thing] gets [done] every [time], without you.` → "Overdue invoices get chased every morning, without you."
3. **Problem as a plain truth the reader nods at.** Use two short sentences, with no drama. → "Unpaid invoices don't age well. The longer a reminder waits, the harder the talk gets."
4. **How it runs:** 4–6 steps. Each one is a short title plus one line, and says the effort. → "Connect Stripe. One click, and you're done."
5. **The rules, in the reader's own words, in quotes.** This shows how easy control is. → 'Be warm. Wait 5 days. Flag anything over $2,000 for me.'
6. **What lands on your desk:** the actual output, word for word, like an email, report or message. Use realistic names and amounts, labelled as an example.
7. **What you get:** 3–4 outcome bullets, each with a number or a time. → "Every late invoice gets a reminder within one business day."
8. **Works with:** the tools it connects to.
9. **Your first month:** a timeline (Day 0 / Day 1 / Week 1 / Month 1) showing what happens and how much effort it takes. → "Day 0: You connect Stripe and set the rules. Five minutes."
10. **Related jobs:** 3 cards in the same job format.

## Pricing page

1. **H1:** say the price shape plainly. → "Simple pricing. Everything included."
2. **Put the price in the first sentence**, with what it buys. → "Outrank costs $99 a month. That gets you 30 articles…"
3. **One plan if you can.** Fewer choices means faster decisions.
4. **Feature list:** write each line as `[Feature], so [benefit]`. → "A different model per teammate, so each job runs on the one that suits it."
5. **Risk reducers next to the price:** the guarantee, with its exact terms ("7-day money-back. Email us within 7 days for a full refund. We ask for one line on what didn't work."), plus "Cancel any time."
6. **"Is [price] all I pay?" FAQ:** list every other cost, including third-party ones, and say which ones are optional.
7. **Add-ons in a table:** name, price, billing and what it does, so nothing is hidden.
8. **Value per unit against the alternative:** → "$99 for 30 finished articles, not credits where the writing is still your job." Only use this if it's true and you can check it.

## Comparison page ("[Us] vs [Them]")

1. **H1:** `[Us] vs [Them]`.
2. **Intro:** describe the competitor fairly, including what it's best at. Then say how you differ.
3. **Price comparison in plain units:** what each costs and what you get for it.
4. **Feature table with a trust line:** "Every claim below is on both products' public pages. Last checked [month, year]."
5. **"Where [Us] wins"** and **"Where [Them] wins"**. Give them real credit.
6. **"Choose [Us] if…"** and **"Choose [Them] if…"**. Help the reader pick, even if they pick the other one.

Honesty is what makes this page work. One unfair claim undoes the whole page.

## Case study

1. **H1, before → after with a number:** `From [before] to [after number].` → "From no SEO plan to 2,213 AI citations."
2. **Summary:** the customer's starting point, what the product did, and the result with numbers.
3. **Stat tiles:** each number with its source and date range. → "Domain Rating 22 → 43 · Ahrefs, June 2025 → Sept 2026"
4. **Sections:** The challenge · What it did · The results · The screenshot · In their words · The honest reading (what the numbers do and don't prove) · Data and method.
5. **Quote:** one real line from the customer, with their name, role and company.

Only use a case study for a real customer, with their permission. If you have none yet, don't make this page.

## Testimonials

- **Label them honestly:** "Unedited notes from founders who use it."
- **Bold the key phrase** in each quote, so readers can scan.
- **Give each person a specific identity:** role, business, place and scale ("Runs a childcare and a contracting business", "Madrid dental clinic since 1945").
- **Real quotes only.** Never polish a quote into something the customer didn't say.

## About page

1. **H1, founders plus proof they use it:** → "Three founders who run their own companies on it."
2. **Why it exists, in two sentences.**
3. **Bios in the third person**, with concrete facts: what they built, ran or sold.
4. **Origin story:** one specific moment with real numbers ("He posted it on X. 3.9 million views later, every founder they knew wanted one.").
5. **Name changes:** if you renamed, say so plainly. → "Same product, same team, a name that finally says what it is."

## Product demo or screenshot

- **Fill it with realistic, labelled demo data:** a named fictional business, real-looking tasks, times and amounts. → "This is Harbor & Pine, a demo workspace. Click around, everything works."
- **Make status lines concrete:** "Drafting reply to Marisol Peters · 24m ago" beats "Processing…".

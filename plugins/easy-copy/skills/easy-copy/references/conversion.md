# Conversion playbook

Copy that's easy to read still has to make someone act. Use this alongside the formulas.

## 1. Find the outcome before you write a word

Climb the **benefit ladder** for every feature. Keep asking "so what?" until you reach something the reader feels:

| Rung | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Feature (what it is) | Reads 12 months of invoices |
| Benefit (what it does for you) | Shows which customers stopped buying |
| Outcome (what changes in your life) | Your regulars order again, and you get more repeat sales |
| Emotion (how it feels) | You stop worrying about who you forgot to call |

- **Headlines use the outcome. Body copy uses the benefit. The feature goes in a short proof line underneath.**
- Write benefits with "so you can": "It writes the message, **so you can** send it in one tap."
- Lead with the gain the reader wants most. If the pain is sharper than the gain, lead with the pain and name the gain right after.

## 2. Pick a frame for the page or section

| Frame | Shape | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PAS** | Problem → Agitate (what it costs) → Solution | Problem sections, emails, ads |
| **BAB** | Before → After → Bridge (the product) | Hero, short ads, social posts |
| **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | Long pages and sales emails |
| **4Ps** | Promise → Picture → Proof → Push | Landing pages with real proof |

Keep "agitate" honest. Show a real cost ("a café stops ordering and nobody notices for 2 months"). No fear-mongering.

## 3. Headlines

- **Write 10 options, then pick one.** Score each 1–4 on the **4 U's**:
  - **Useful:** the reader gets something.
  - **Urgent:** there's a reason to act now. Only use honest reasons.
  - **Unique:** only you could say it.
  - **Ultra-specific:** it has a number, a name or a concrete case.
- Keep the two best options as A/B test variants.
- Ten words or fewer. The reader should be able to repeat it after one read.
- Message match: the headline repeats the promise from the ad, email or link that brought the reader there.
- Put the reader in it: "you", "your customers", "your invoices".

## 4. Above the fold (first screen)

The first screen must answer these four questions without scrolling:
1. **What is it?** Say it in plain words.
2. **Who is it for?** Name the reader or their business.
3. **What do I get?** The outcome.
4. **What do I do next?** One primary button.

Under the button, add 2–3 small risk reducers: "Free to join · No card needed · Cancel any time". Only use ones that are true.

## 5. Calls to action

- **One page, one goal.** One primary action. Repeat the same button after every major section. Secondary links ("How it works") are quieter.
- **The button says what happens, in the reader's voice:** "Get my free audit", "Start my trial", "Join the waitlist". Never use "Submit", "Learn more" or "Click here".
- **First person often wins on buttons:** "Get **my** audit" usually beats "Get **your** audit". Test it.
- **Next to the button, answer the last worry:** "Takes 30 seconds", "No card needed", "We never share your email".
- **The final CTA restates the outcome** and then gives the button.

## 6. Proof and trust

- **Only real proof:** named customers, real numbers, real quotes, real logos. Never invent any of them. If there's no proof yet, say what's honestly true ("We're testing it now on real invoices") and leave the proof section out.
- **Specific beats impressive:** "Cut review time from 30 minutes to 8" beats "Saves tons of time".
- **Put proof near the claim it supports,** not only in a testimonial block at the bottom.
- **Trust lines answer fears in one sentence:** "Nothing reaches a customer without your yes." "We only read your data. We never change it."

- **Show the output.** With no customers yet, the most convincing proof is the real thing the product makes: an email, a list or a report, word for word, labelled as an example.
- **Proof numbers bar:** 3–4 big real numbers ("750m+ views", "25,000 backlinks added"), each one checkable. Leave it out until the numbers are real.
- **Show that you use it yourself.** "We run our own companies on it" is proof when it's true.
- **Show your working.** Case-study numbers need their source and dates ("Ahrefs, June 2025 → Sept 2026"). Add an honest reading of what the numbers do and don't prove.
- **Be fair about competitors.** On comparison pages, say where they win and who should pick them, and date your claims. Fairness makes the rest believable. See `references/page-types.md`.

## 7. Objections

- **List the top 5 reasons a reader would say no:** too expensive, too hard to set up, won't work for my business, not now, don't trust it.
- **Answer each one where it comes up:** next to the button, in a card, or in the FAQ.
- **FAQ answers open with Yes or No,** then give one line of why and one line of what to do.

## 7b. Pricing copy

- **Put the price in the first sentence** of the pricing page and of the "How much does it cost?" answer.
- **One plan, everything included,** when you can. If there are add-ons, list them in a table with their prices.
- **Answer "Is [price] all I pay?" completely,** including third-party costs, and say which costs are optional.
- **Spell out the guarantee:** how many days, how to claim, and what you ask in return.
- **Write each feature as `[feature], so [benefit]`.**

## 8. Urgency and scarcity (honest only)

- **Use real limits only:** "20 founding spots", "price locked for early users", "offer ends Friday". Only when they're true.
- **No fake timers, no fake stock counts, no "only 2 left" unless it's real.**
- **Explaining why acting now matters is still allowed:** "Every week you wait, another customer quietly stops ordering."

## 9. Scannability

- Most readers only scan. Put the meaning in the headings, the first line of each section, bold labels and button text.
- **Paragraphs are 1–3 sentences.** Add a subhead every 3–4 short paragraphs.
- Use lists for parallel items. Use tables for comparisons. Keep prose for stories and reasons.
- **Lead with the most important word.** "Unpaid invoices: …" scans better than "When it comes to invoices that haven't been paid…"

## 10. Voice of customer

- **Borrow the reader's exact words** from calls, reviews, support tickets, Reddit and sales notes. "Good customers go quiet" beats "customer attrition".
- **Keep a short list of their phrases** in the project's context file and reuse them in headlines.

## 11. Before you ship

- **Read it out loud.** Anything you stumble on, rewrite.
- **Run the 5-second test on someone new:** show the first screen for 5 seconds, then ask what it is and who it's for.
- **Plan one test:** headline A vs B, or button A vs B. Write the variants now.
